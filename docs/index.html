Luma

A multitrack audio mixer for Linux. Play many tracks at once and control each one your way: volume, balance, fades, effects and more.

GitHub Flathub

What is Luma

Luma is like a mixing desk on your computer. Each audio track (a song, a sound effect, a voice-over…) sits on its own channel, and you decide how it sounds and when: raise or lower its volume, move it left or right, add effects, fade it in or out, and send it to one set of speakers or another.

It is designed for live shows, radio, theatre, events, or any situation where you need to launch and mix several audios reliably. You don't need any technical knowledge to use it: this guide walks you through it step by step.

1Getting started

The first time you open Luma you'll see the welcome screen. From here you choose what to work on:

  • New project: creates an empty project. Just give it a name and choose where to save it.
  • Open project: opens a project you already have.
  • Recent projects: quick access to what you opened last. The ✕ on each row removes it from the list (you'll be asked to confirm; it does not delete anything from your disk, only the shortcut).
  • About: information about the app (version, license).

Next time you open Luma, it will automatically reopen the last project you were working on (you can change this in Preferences).

2Projects

A project holds all your work: the tracks, each channel's settings, the presets and the flows. Each project is a folder that you can copy, move or take to another computer without worry.

The key point: when you add a track, Luma keeps a copy inside the project. So even if you delete or move the original file, your project keeps sounding the same. The folder is self-contained and portable.

If you double-click a .luma file in your file manager, it opens directly in Luma.

3The main window

The screen is divided into three areas:

  • Top: the bar with the project, the overall volume and the presets.
  • Left: the track list (top) and the flow list (bottom).
  • Right: the channels, which is where you do the mixing.
You can move the window by dragging any empty area of the top bar, and resize it from the edges.

4The top bar

  • PROJECTS: closes the current project and returns to the welcome screen.
  • MASTER: the overall volume of the whole mix, with its level meter.
  • PANIC: the emergency button. Stops all audio instantly.
  • Options buttons: audio outputs, number of channels, reorder, grid and preferences.
  • Preset buttons (P1…P10): switch between your saved scenes.

5Track list

This is your project's audio library (top left).

  • Add files: import audio in WAV, MP3, OGG or FLAC format. They are sorted automatically in alphabetical order.
  • For each track you see its name, duration, format and which channels it's used in.
  • To use a track: drag it onto a channel, or use its menu and choose Bind to assign it.
  • The menu also has Remove to take it out (it asks for confirmation and deletes the project's copy).

6Channels

Each channel is a column that plays one track. From top to bottom you have:

  • Colour bar and name (double-click to rename it). The colour helps you spot channels at a glance.
  • Balance: moves the sound to the left or right.
  • Volume: the large slider, with its level meter. It has two markers that set how far fades go up or down.
  • Fades: buttons to make the track appear (fade in) or disappear (fade out) smoothly. You can set how many seconds it lasts and the type of curve.
  • AUX: sends this channel to the auxiliary output instead of the main one.
  • Effects and Equalizer: open their panels (sections 7 and 8). They light up when active.
  • Repeat: plays the track in a loop.
  • Play / Pause and Stop.
  • Progress bar: shows where the track is; click it to trim the part to play (section 9).
Right-click a channel to assign or remove its track and change its colour quickly.

7Effects

Each channel has five effects you can switch on independently. Each one remembers its values even when off, and there's a button to return to the defaults.

8Equalizer

The equalizer lets you boost or cut different frequencies of the sound: the lows, the mids and the highs. It has 10 bands (from 32 Hz to 16 kHz) and each one goes up or down by up to ±12 dB.

For example: lower the lowest bands to remove rumble, or raise the high ones for more brightness.

9Trimming a track

If you only want to play part of a track, click its progress bar. A window opens where you can:

  • See the total duration and the current position.
  • Set a start and an end to play only that part.
  • Clear the start or end trim, or reset to the full track.

10Presets (scenes)

A preset is a complete snapshot of the whole desk: which track each channel has, its volumes, effects, colours… You have 10 presets (P1 to P10) per project.

They're handy for preparing different moments of an event. For example: P1 for the audience walk-in, P2 for the main act, P3 for the closing. You switch between them with a click.

Switching preset stops the current playback (you'll be asked to confirm) and Luma automatically saves the state of the preset you're leaving, so you don't lose anything.

11Flows

Flows automate playback: instead of launching each track by hand, you define a sequence and Luma runs it for you. Ideal for chaining audios with smooth transitions.

Creating a flow

From the flow list (bottom left) press New flow. An editor opens with a canvas where you build the sequence like a small map:

  • Add steps: click a channel in the list to place it on the canvas as a “node”. The same channel can appear several times.
  • Connect them: drag from a node's output point to the next one to link them. The START point marks where it begins.
  • Move around the canvas: drag the background or use the wheel; Ctrl + wheel to zoom in/out.

Configuring each step

Double-click a node to decide how that channel should sound when the flow activates it (volume, fade, loop, start point, effects…).

Click a link to decide when it moves to the next step and what happens:

  • Trigger: immediately, after a few seconds, or when the previous track finishes.
  • To the origin: do nothing, fade out or stop.
  • To the target: play directly or come in with a fade in.

Running

Press Run to launch the flow. The channels involved show a blinking border so you can identify them, and you can keep touching the desk while it runs. The flow ends on its own, or with Stop or PANIC; when it finishes, the channels return to how they were.

12Audio outputs

Luma can send the sound to two different outputs:

  • Main: where most channels go.
  • Aux: where the channels with the AUX button enabled go.

In the outputs dialog you choose which device each one uses (for example, speakers for the audience and headphones for you), or leave the system default device.

13Mixer settings

  • Number of channels: choose how many channels you want (from 1 to 40). If you reduce the number, you'll be warned that you'll lose the configuration of the last ones.
  • Reorder channels: turns on a mode to change the order of the channels.
  • Grid mode: lays out the channels in two rows, handy when you have many.

14Preferences

  • Open the last project on startup: turn on or off whether Luma resumes your work automatically.
  • Language: change the interface language (applied after restart). Available in English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch.
  • Audio cache size and rewarm cache: performance settings so tracks play without delays.
  • Keyboard shortcuts: you can reassign any shortcut. Press its button and then the key combination you prefer.
